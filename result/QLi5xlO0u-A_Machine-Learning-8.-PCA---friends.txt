What is probably worth mentioning about all these algorithms like PCA, TSNE, ISOMAP, MBS, UMAP is that they are often used to help you visualize your data well, right? You can't really look at a high dimensional space with your own eyes, but you want to see how close your data points are to each other in that full space, or how far apart they are.
That is why you want to use something like TSNE or UMAP so that it clusters your original data in this way. Basically, the better the clustering with these methods, the more easily you can solve the task with simple algorithms because there is no need to account for complex local point structures or use deep learning methods.
So in essence, if you get some random input data, it is very useful to run it through UMAP or TSNE to take a look at the visualization of that data. If the clustering is good, you can use fairly simple methods.
If the clustering is poor, then either you might have to use very deep models, or the task is simply poorly solvable. None of these are algorithms. By deep learning algorithms, I mean well, deep networks, or doom crash.
